— a note from the desk —

Poetry in Motion is a small, slow journal of moving poems. Each piece is a short text set against a single looped image: the tide, the static, a cloud that will not leave the window.

We publish irregularly. We believe a poem is a kind of weather you walk into and out of, and that the right film loop — chosen carefully, played quietly — can be the doorframe. Nothing here is meant to be hurried. Read one. Read it twice. Let the video keep going after you have closed the page.

— the editors

// about

A journal,
set to a single
looping image.

Poetry in Motion began as a private habit: writing short poems to play under a film loop, the way other people score films with music. We kept doing it. Eventually it had a name.

Every issue is one poem and one moving image, chosen so that neither describes the other. The film is not an illustration. The poem is not a caption. They are two strangers seated at the same long table, waiting for the soup.

We are a small editorial of two. We read on Sundays. We reply, eventually. We do not pay in money — we pay in a careful page, a film chosen with you, and a permanent place in the index.

If you would like to submit, write to the address printed at the bottom of every poem. Please attach nothing. Paste the poem into the body of the letter, the way one used to.
